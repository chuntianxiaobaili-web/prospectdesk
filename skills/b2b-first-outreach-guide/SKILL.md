# First-outreach writing guide

Produce a Chinese guide for proactive first contact with a business that has no prior correspondence. This is teaching and review material, not an inquiry reply, a bulk-mail template or a sending workflow.

Use existing knowledge. Do not browse, collect a source corpus or cite sources. Do not invent statistics, response rates, customer results or training completion. This authored guide does not count as 100 externally reviewed learning sources.

Follow the five sections in [the Chinese working guide](references/writing-guide.zh-CN.md):
1. Decide what customer and seller information matters, what can be omitted, and what must be verified.
2. Demonstrate customer fact → possible buying consideration → supported offer for wholesalers, retailers and project buyers. Label the middle step as inference.
3. Explain subject, opening, one proposal, one credible detail and a single easy next-step question; explain common errors with natural English examples.
4. Show complete before-and-after English drafts for one explicitly fictional case, using the same facts in both. Do not strengthen the rewrite through fabricated prices, lead times, certifications or buyer demand.
5. Supply a per-message checklist and a company-name substitution test that points back to the fact, recommendation or question needing a rewrite.

Separate common principles from choices that depend on industry, buyer role, offering and context. When teaching digital products or services, use verified license/compatibility/delivery or scope/deliverable/collaboration details instead of inventing physical samples or stock. If the intended audience is not B2B, explain the corresponding first-contact framing and required recipient context without claiming the sales plugin supports a new recipient schema.

Keep evidence and reviewer comments out of customer-visible example bodies. Clearly label all fictional company facts. Do not log into mail, create mailbox drafts, reply to inquiries, schedule or send.
